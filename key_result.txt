PhD Thesis: Quantum light driven many level systems in the weak and strong coupling limits
Author: Shahabed Chatraee Azizabadi
Institution: Technische Universität Berlin, 2017

================================================================================
MAIN RESEARCH TOPIC
================================================================================

Investigation of open quantum systems consisting of coupled optical cavities 
interacting with multi-level quantum emitters (two-level atoms or excitons) 
under incoherent driving. The work focuses on the transition between weak and 
strong light-matter coupling regimes and the control of quantum correlations 
and photon statistics.

Core Setup:
- Source cavity: Incoherently pumped
- Target cavity: Contains one or more two-level systems (atoms/excitons)
- Coupling: Jaynes-Cummings type interaction between cavity modes and emitters
- Driving: Incoherent pumping (rate Γ) on the source emitter

================================================================================
KEY PHYSICAL SYSTEMS STUDIED
================================================================================

1. Single source cavity with a driven two-level emitter
2. Coupled source-target cavities with one emitter in the target
3. Coupled cavities with two emitters (atoms or excitons) in the target
4. Systems with three emitters in the target cavity

Two coupling models were compared:
- Atomic model: Standard Jaynes-Cummings coupling
- Excitonic model: Modified coupling terms with different dissipation channels

================================================================================
METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

- Hamiltonian: Jaynes-Cummings model for cavity-emitter interaction
- Open system dynamics: Lindblad master equation
- Numerical framework: QuTiP (Quantum Toolbox in Python)
- Solution method: Steady-state solution of the Liouvillian superoperator
- Observables:
  * Average photon number ⟨n⟩ = ⟨a†a⟩
  * Second-order correlation function g²(0)
  * Wigner function of the cavity field
  * Concurrence (for two-qubit entanglement)
  * Von Neumann entropy (mixedness/entanglement measure)

Parameter regimes explored:
- Weak to strong coupling (g/κ, g/γ)
- Different incoherent pump rates Γ
- Varying cavity decay rates (κ_s, κ_t)
- Different emitter-cavity coupling strengths (g_s, g_t)

================================================================================
KEY RESULTS
================================================================================

1. Control of Target Photon Statistics via Source Pumping
   - The photon statistics (thermal vs. antibunched) of the target cavity 
     can be influenced by the incoherent pumping rate applied to the source 
     cavity emitter.
   - Increasing the source pump rate Γ can drive the target system from 
     thermal (g²(0) > 1) toward coherent or antibunched regimes.

2. Atomic vs Excitonic Coupling Models
   - Significant differences were observed between atomic and excitonic 
     coupling in terms of:
       * Steady-state photon number
       * Photon blockade / bunching behavior
       * Generation of entanglement between emitters
   - Excitonic models showed modified cross-damping terms and different 
     effective coupling strengths.

3. Indirect vs Direct Coupling
   - Indirect coupling (mediated by the cavity field) vs direct dipole-dipole 
     coupling between emitters leads to qualitatively different steady-state 
     properties and entanglement dynamics.
   - Cavity-mediated (indirect) coupling can generate entanglement even in 
     the presence of strong dissipation.

4. Entanglement Generation and Quantification
   - Concurrence and von Neumann entropy were used to quantify entanglement 
     between two emitters in the target cavity.
   - Entanglement was found to depend strongly on the pump rate, detuning, 
     and the ratio g_t / κ_t.
   - Optimal parameter regimes for maximum entanglement were identified.

5. Wigner Function Analysis
   - Wigner functions of the cavity field revealed non-classical features 
     (negative regions) under specific driving conditions.
   - Transition from thermal to non-classical states was observed as 
     function of pump strength and coupling parameters.

6. Hierarchical and Multi-Emitter Systems
   - Systems with two and three emitters in the target cavity were studied.
   - Collective effects and modified photon statistics were analyzed.
   - Scaling behavior of entanglement and photon correlations with emitter 
     number was investigated.
